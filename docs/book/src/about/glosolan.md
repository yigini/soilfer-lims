# GLOSOLAN & the Global Soil Partnership

## What Is GLOSOLAN?

**GLOSOLAN** (Global Soil Laboratory Network) is a network of soil laboratories from around the world, coordinated by the **FAO Global Soil Partnership (GSP)**. Established in 2017, it brings together approximately **1,000 laboratories across 155 countries** — making it the largest soil laboratory network on the planet.

The core challenge GLOSOLAN addresses is this: if a soil laboratory in Kenya measures pH using one method, and a lab in Guatemala measures pH using a different method, the results can't be directly compared. This makes it impossible to create reliable global or regional soil maps. GLOSOLAN solves this by harmonizing how soil is analyzed worldwide.

## Why Does GLOSOLAN Matter?

Imagine you're building a map of soil fertility across Africa. You need data from hundreds of laboratories in dozens of countries. But each lab uses slightly different equipment, different chemical methods, and different quality standards. The data is inconsistent and unreliable.

GLOSOLAN exists to fix this by:

- **📋 Standardizing methods** — developing Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) so every lab follows the same protocols
- **📊 Quality assurance** — running proficiency testing programs where labs analyze the same reference samples and compare results
- **🎓 Training** — providing capacity building so laboratory staff can perform analyses correctly and consistently
- **📐 Equipment guidance** — advising on proper equipment maintenance and calibration
- **🔬 Soil spectroscopy** — advancing the use of infrared spectroscopy as a fast, affordable soil analysis technique

## GLOSOLAN's Regional Networks

GLOSOLAN operates through six regional networks, each coordinating laboratories in their part of the world:

| Regional Network | Coverage |
|-----------------|----------|
| **AFRILAB** | Africa |
| **ASOLASIN** | Asia |
| **EUROSOLAN** | Europe |
| **NASOLAN** | North America |
| **SOLAN-LAC** | Latin America & Caribbean |
| **SEALNET** | Southeast Asia |

Each regional network organizes training, proficiency testing, and capacity building adapted to the specific needs and contexts of their member laboratories.

## How Is GLOSOLAN Connected to SoilFER-LIMS?

SoilFER-LIMS was developed as part of the broader SoilFER programme, which relies on GLOSOLAN for:

1. **Building laboratory capacity** — GLOSOLAN trains the lab technicians who will use SoilFER-LIMS daily
2. **Establishing national lab networks** — In SoilFER countries, national soil laboratory networks are created under the GLOSOLAN umbrella (e.g., GHASOLAN in Ghana, GUASOLAN in Guatemala)
3. **Quality standards** — The QC workflows in SoilFER-LIMS are designed to support the quality assurance standards promoted by GLOSOLAN
4. **Harmonized data** — When all labs in a country use the same LIMS with the same SOPs, the resulting data is consistent and can feed into national and global soil databases

## The Global Soil Partnership (GSP)

GLOSOLAN is one of several initiatives under the **Global Soil Partnership**, a voluntary partnership hosted by the FAO that was established in 2012. The GSP's mission is to promote sustainable soil management and improve soil governance worldwide.

Other important GSP initiatives include:

| Initiative | What It Does |
|-----------|-------------|
| **INSII** | International Network of Soil Information Institutions — coordinates soil data sharing |
| **GSOCmap** | Global Soil Organic Carbon Map — first global map of soil carbon, created from data provided by 110+ countries |
| **Voluntary Guidelines for SSM** | Framework adopted by FAO member states for sustainable soil management |
| **World Soil Day** | December 5th — annual event raising awareness about soil health |
