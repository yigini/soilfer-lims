01 Where the laboratory stops

You asked that the laboratory keep laboratory things only, with the estimation service running as an independent platform. That is the right division, and it is worth stating precisely, because the boundary determines the data model rather than the other way round.

Two rules that follow

  • The LIMS never stores a preprocessed spectrum. Every preprocessing choice — SNV, MSC, a Savitzky–Golay window, a continuum removal — belongs to a specific model, is irreversible, and will be revised. Store what the instrument produced; let the estimation service transform its own copy.
  • Everything the estimation service needs is metadata, not computation. That is what makes the boundary cheap to hold. The laboratory does not need to know what a Kubelka–Munk transform is; it needs to record whether the numbers are reflectance or absorbance.

What that buys

  • The estimation team can rebuild every model from scratch at any time, because the raw file and the acquisition conditions survive.
  • A model failure is diagnosable — you can ask whether the instrument drifted, whether the background was stale, whether the samples were ground differently.
  • The library keeps its value when the estimation platform is replaced, which on a ten-year horizon it will be.

02 Current status

Eighteen findings, traced in source. The infrastructure is better than it looks in places — there is an API-key mechanism, a scoped external endpoint, a delta-sync, a review workflow and an audit trail. What is missing is almost entirely on the scientific side of the record.

SPEC-01

The library endpoint throws on every requestCritical

scopeGuard is referenced at spectralController.js:53 and imported nowhere in scope. Caught, logged, returned as 500. The whole page is dark.

SPEC-02

The instrument file is never keptCritical

The client parses the CSV in the browser and posts two arrays of numbers. The server stores those arrays and discards everything else. There is no copy of what the instrument wrote.

This is the defect with the longest tail. A parsing bug found in a year cannot be corrected retrospectively. An OPUS or JCAMP file carries resolution, apodization, co-added scans, laser wavenumber, detector, beamsplitter and the background reference — all of it is thrown away at the browser. And an assessor asking to see the primary record cannot be answered.

SPEC-03

Nothing records what the numbers areCritical

There is no field anywhere for reflectance, absorbance, pseudo-absorbance log(1/R), Kubelka–Munk or transmittance. modality holds NIR or MIR, which is the spectral region, not the quantity.

A consumer receiving [0.412, 0.418, …] cannot tell whether to apply log(1/R). Guessing from magnitude works until it does not: a well-baselined DRIFT absorbance spectrum of a sandy soil and a reflectance spectrum of a dark clay occupy the same numeric range. This single field is the difference between a library and a pile of numbers.

SPEC-04

Every spectrum is force-sorted into ascending axis orderHigh

spectralController.js:361–364 sorts the axis ascending before validation and before storage. For NIR in nanometres that is correct and harmless. For mid-infrared it inverts the convention every instrument, every journal and every technician uses — see section 03.

SPEC-05

The instrument is a free-text string, usually "Unknown"High

Stored as metadata.instrument inside a JSON blob, defaulting to 'Unknown'. Meanwhile the schema already carries EquipmentAsset, EquipmentQualification, EquipmentEvent and WorkItemEquipmentUse — a full instrument register with calibration history, unreferenced by any spectrum.

There is also a scanIp column on SpectralData. A network address is not an instrument identity.

SPEC-06

CSV only, parsed in the browserHigh

Both upload components declare accept=".csv" and reject anything else. No JCAMP-DX, no OPUS, no SPC, no ASD binary, no ENVI.

So every scan passes through a manual export in vendor software, where an operator chooses — silently, per export — whether to write reflectance or absorbance, at what resolution, over what range, with what interpolation. That export step is where the metadata dies, and it is also the step no one records.

SPEC-07

No acquisition or preparation conditionsHigh

Missing: spectral resolution, number of co-added scans, accessory (DRIFT / ATR / integrating sphere), background reference and when it was collected, detector, beamsplitter, aperture; and on the sample side, grinding fineness, sieve fraction, moisture state, cup or window material, and which replicate this is.

Grinding alone moves mid-infrared band intensities by more than the difference between two soils. A library that cannot separate "different soil" from "differently ground" cannot support a transferable calibration.

SPEC-08

The external endpoint applies no laboratory scopingHigh

sisController.getSpectra reads req.sisAuth?.labs. apiKeyAuth sets type, name, role, countries, projects — never labs, and the ApiKey model has no such column. So keyLabs is always undefined, hasGlobalLab is always true, and the laboratory filter never applies. Country scoping still bites when a key sets it; a key without countries reads every laboratory's spectra.

SPEC-09

The external payload carries no metadata and no chemistryHigh

The response is id, sampleId, labId, modality, filename, timestamp, qcStatus and two arrays. No instrument, no resolution, no quantity, no preparation, no basis — and no reference values.

The pairing of a spectrum with an approved wet-chemistry result is the entire asset. Today the estimation team must call /results separately and join on a key whose semantics are undocumented, with no indication of which method or unit produced each reference number.

SPEC-10

The QC is array validation, not spectroscopyMedium

spectralValidation.js checks length, monotonicity, range, flatness, negatives and a mean successive difference. Those are worth having. But the thresholds are blind to the quantity — maxVal > 1.2 flags any honest absorbance spectrum — and mad > 0.05 is a constant compared against a quantity that scales with spectral resolution and with whether the values are R or A. A 2 cm⁻¹ scan and a 16 cm⁻¹ scan of the same soil get different verdicts.

What a spectroscopist looks for is absent: local saturation, atmospheric CO₂ and water-vapour residuals, detector splice steps, baseline slope, replicate agreement, and distance from the existing library. Section 05 sets these out.

SPEC-11

The instrument-range override is never passedMedium

WP-33 added an options.instrumentRange parameter so a per-instrument window could override the global one. The only call site is validateSpectra(sortedWavelengths, sortedValues, scanItem.modality) — three arguments. The fourth is never supplied, so the feature has never run.

SPEC-12

The checksum helper is defined and never calledMedium

calculateChecksum sits at line 7 with zero call sites. Consequences: the same file uploaded twice produces two records, nothing detects a truncated or corrupted transfer, and there is no content identity to give the estimation service for cache validation.

SPEC-13

Sample matching falls back to an unscoped 500-row scanMedium

When exact and by-id lookups fail, the controller runs prisma.sample.findMany({ take: 500 }) with no filter and no ordering, then matches case-insensitively in JavaScript. Past 500 samples the match silently stops working, and which 500 is undefined. A mismatched laboratory ID is only a warning — the scan is attached to the sample anyway.

SPEC-14

Uploading a spectrum silently changes sample and workflow stateMedium

The upload writes sample.assignedLab when it finds an unassigned sample, and sets a work item to COMPLETED with a direct prisma.workItem.update — neither through transitionSample. The work item is chosen by fuzzy string match (wAnalysis.includes('NIR')) rather than from the catalogue.

None of the per-scan writes share a transaction, so a failure mid-batch leaves a spectrum with no audit row, or a completed work item with no spectrum.

SPEC-15

The comparison overlay matches wavelengths by float equalityMedium

SpectraViewer.jsx:34 builds the union of all axis values, then for each scan does chartData.find(p => p.wavelength === w). Exact equality across two instruments, or two exports at different resolutions, almost never matches — the overlay renders as gaps. It is also quadratic: two 1750-point spectra cost about six million comparisons.

SPEC-16

A manager can auto-approve their own uploadMedium

autoApprove arrives in the request body and is honoured for LAB_MANAGER and SUPER_ADMIN, writing reviewedBy = uploader. For a reference library that will feed an external calibration, self-review is a governance gap rather than a bug — but it should be a conscious laboratory setting, not a request parameter.

SPEC-17

Spectra are stored as JSON text and the list is unpaginatedLow

Two arrays serialised as JSON strings run roughly 80–120 KB per mid-infrared scan, parsed in JavaScript on every read. getLibrary has no take at all, and the page computes its statistics client-side over the whole result. At a few hundred scans this is invisible; at ten thousand it is the page.

SPEC-18

The y-axis is labelled "absorbance" whatever it holdsLow

getScan maps every value into a field named absorbance and the chart labels it so. External pagination is also unstable: orderBy: timestamp desc with skip/take and no tiebreaker means a concurrent upload shifts rows and the consumer silently misses records.

03 The axis, and why it matters more than it looks

A sort that looks like tidying is the clearest example of the gap between handling arrays and handling spectra.

Fix the storage, not the plot. Keep the axis exactly as the instrument delivered it and record its direction; let the viewer decide how to draw. Sorting at ingest to make a chart library happy is how a data layer acquires a presentation bug permanently.

04 What the record has to carry

05 Quality control a spectroscopist expects

These are checks on the measurement, which is laboratory work. Chemometric outlier detection against a calibration model is not — that stays on the other side of the boundary. The one exception is distance from the library itself, which is curation.

CheckWhat it catchesHow
Local saturation Detector at full scale over a strong band — the current flat-signal check only fires if the whole spectrum is flat Any run of ≥ 5 consecutive identical values, or values pinned at the instrument maximum
Atmospheric residual A stale or poor background: the commonest FTIR failure in a working laboratory Sharp structure at 2350 cm⁻¹ (CO₂) and regular rotational fine structure in 3900–3500 and 1900–1300 cm⁻¹
Detector splice Step discontinuity where a vis-NIR instrument changes detector Step at 1000 nm and 1830 nm exceeding local noise by a set factor — the commonest ASD defect
Noise, properly Poor packing, too few co-adds, a failing source RMS of the residual after a Savitzky–Golay smooth, measured in a signal-free window (2200–1900 cm⁻¹ for MIR), scaled by resolution — not a fixed successive-difference constant
Baseline slope and offset Dirty window, poor DRIFT packing, specular reflection Linear fit across the span; flag beyond a per-accessory tolerance
Replicate agreement Sample heterogeneity and packing error — the honest estimate of measurement uncertainty RMS difference between independent repacks; flag above a per-instrument limit
Distance from the library Wrong sample, empty cup, the background scanned by mistake, a mislabelled file Mahalanobis distance in the first few principal components of the approved library for that instrument and preparation
Instrument stability Drift between sessions — the thing that quietly invalidates a calibration A control sample scanned each session, tracked on a chart with limits, as ASTM E1421 does for FTIR performance

Thresholds belong to the instrument, not to the code. Every number above differs between a benchtop FTIR with a DRIFT accessory and a field spectroradiometer. They belong on the EquipmentAsset record, set once during instrument commissioning — which is also what finally gives options.instrumentRange (SPEC-11) a caller.

06 The contract with the estimation service

The foundation exists: /api/v1/data-exchange/spectra, API-key authentication with hashed keys and expiry, country and project scoping, delta sync. Four things have to change before another team can build on it.

1 · Fix the scoping hole first

Add labs to ApiKey, populate req.sisAuth.labs in apiKeyAuth, and default to deny rather than to global when the field is absent. Until then a key with no country restriction reads every laboratory's spectra (SPEC-08).

2 · Return the calibration pair, not the spectrum alone

A spectrum without its reference value is not training data. The endpoint should offer both representations from one call:

GET /api/v1/data-exchange/spectra?instrument=FTIR-GTM-01&withReference=SOC,CLAY&cursor=…
{
  "meta": { "cursor": "…", "hasMore": true, "schema": "spectra/v1" },
  "data": [{
    "id": "spec-…", "sha256": "…", "sampleId": "…", "labId": "LAB-GTM",
    "signal":      { "quantity": "ABSORBANCE", "axisUnit": "WAVENUMBER_CM1",
                     "axisDirection": "DESCENDING", "isRaw": true, "nPoints": 1738 },
    "acquisition": { "equipmentId": "eq-…", "model": "Bruker Alpha II",
                     "accessory": "DRIFT", "resolution": 4, "coAddedScans": 32,
                     "background": "GOLD", "scannedAt": "2026-08-14T09:12:00Z" },
    "preparation": { "grinding": "AGATE_100UM", "moistureState": "AIR_DRY",
                     "replicateNo": 1 },
    "qc":          { "status": "PASS", "flags": [], "approvedAt": "…" },
    "reference":   [{ "param": "SOC", "value": 18.4, "unit": "g/kg",
                      "method": "GLOSOLAN-SOP-08 dry combustion",
                      "basis": "OVEN_DRY", "provenance": "MEASURED",
                      "resultId": "res-…" }],
    "axis": [...], "values": [...]
  }]
}

The reference block is what makes this a spectral library rather than a file share. It should carry only MEASURED results in an APPROVED state, with the method and unit that produced them.

3 · Give it a bulk path

Two hundred mid-infrared spectra as inline JSON is roughly 20 MB per page, re-serialised from stored strings on every request. Model training pulls the whole library. Add a job-based export that writes a columnar file — Parquet, or an axis matrix plus a metadata table — and returns a signed URL. Keep the JSON endpoint for browsing and for incremental sync.

4 · Make pagination stable and cacheable

Replace skip/take over timestamp desc with a cursor on (timestamp, id), and expose sha256 as an ETag so a consumer can skip what it already holds. Then publish the whole thing in docs/ beside the NSIS specification, versioned — the estimation team is building against a contract, and a contract that only exists in the controller is not one.

08 What stays out of the LIMS

Worth writing down, because each of these will be proposed at some point by someone reasonable, and each would move the boundary.

Not in the LIMSWhy
Preprocessing pipelines — SNV, MSC, derivatives, continuum removalModel-dependent, irreversible, and revised with every model. Store what the instrument produced.
Calibration models of any kindA separate platform with its own lifecycle, versioning and validation regime. Duplicating it here means two model registries and no authority.
Predicted values written back as resultsA prediction is not a determination. If they are ever displayed, they arrive as separate records marked PREDICTED, never merged into a certificate column.
Calibration transfer between instrumentsNeeds paired standardisation samples and model context. The laboratory's obligation is to scan the transfer set and store it like any other scan.
Resampling to a common axis grid at storageLossy and irreversible. Resample for display only; ship native axes to consumers.
A public spectral library portalDifferent problem: licensing, consent, embargo and citation. Worth doing later, on top of a correct internal library, not instead of one.

One test to carry through. Before any package is marked complete, upload a real instrument file, approve it, pull it back through the external API with a scoped key, and check that every field needed to rebuild the measurement survived the round trip. The three pieces of code in this subsystem that were written and never called — calculateChecksum, the instrument-range override, and the scope-guard import that was never added — all passed review. None of them would have survived that test.
